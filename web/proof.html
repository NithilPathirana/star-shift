How each verdict is decided

Method

  1. Lining up the images. Each SPHEREx image is cut out around the object and redrawn on one north-up grid of 6.15″ pixels, using the image's own sky coordinates.
    more

    The cutout is centred on where the catalogue says the object is on that image's date. The field is 2.5′ (25 × 25 pixels). We use 120 images spread evenly in time, the viewer's default.

  2. Finding the star's centre. In each image we take the brightness-weighted centre of the light within 3.5 pixels (21″), after taking away the sky level measured in a ring around it.
    more

    The first season's images are stacked to find the starting point near the catalogue position. After that, each image is searched where the earlier images say the star should be. An image counts only when the star's peak is more than 5 times the noise and within 2 pixels of that prediction.

  3. Fitting the motion or the brightness change. A straight line through the east and north positions against time gives the motion per year. For brightness, each image is compared with first-season images from the same detector at the same wavelength.
    more

    Before fitting, each position has the yearly parallax wobble taken out (the catalogue parallax and Earth's position on that date). Each image is weighted by how well images of its kind are measured: the scatter of the positions from the same detector, with or without a blanked (saturated) centre. The east and north slopes combine as μ = √(μE² + μN²). Its uncertainty is the scatter about the two lines plus a systematic floor of 0.15″/yr in each direction, added in quadrature. The floor comes from field stars in the same images, measured the same way: they should not move, and their fitted motions scatter that much more than their own error bars allow. A patch with 3 or more usable field stars uses its own floor (never below 0.15″/yr); the others use the 0.15″/yr measured on all five patches together, and each row says which. Faint field stars do not show what a blanked, saturated centre does to a position, so the direction is checked separately: measured minus catalogue motion as an arrow, χ = √((Δe/σe)² + (Δn/σn)²) over 2 degrees of freedom, turned into the one-dimensional σ with the same probability. Points more than 3 times the scatter from the line are dropped once. A motion under 3 times its uncertainty, or under 0.7″/yr, is reported as “no significant motion”, with an upper limit (value + 2 uncertainties, at least 0.7″/yr). The 0.7″/yr line is set by RW Aurigae, which hardly moves but gives 0.5–0.6″/yr depending on which flagged images are hidden. Brightness is the light within 15″ of the catalogue position; a change is Δm = −2.5 log₁₀(F / Ffirst season), measured separately below and above 3.8 µm.

  4. Leaving out flagged pixels. Pixels the archive marks as bad (saturated, hot, sudden jumps, afterglow) are blanked before measuring. Whole images are kept, as in the viewer's default.
    more

    Each row's evidence also shows the result when every image with a flag near the star is hidden. For brightness, an image is left out if any pixel within 15″ of the star is blanked, because a blanked centre would make the star look fainter.

  5. The viewer's measurement set. The table uses the viewer's own code and the same 120 images it measures, spread evenly in time, across all detectors (detectors 5–6 only for WISE 0855−0714), with no flag boxes ticked, so no image is hidden. The viewer's movie may play just one band at a time for viewing (its counter then shows fewer images); the measurement still uses all 120. If you tick flag boxes in the viewer, its measured row says how many images it used and that flagged ones were hidden.
  6. Was there a bias? We checked. An earlier version came out low: Barnard's Star 9.75 ± 0.54″/yr, Epsilon Indi B 4.37 ± 0.09″/yr (3.6σ below Gaia), and RW Aurigae, which hardly moves, 0.57 ± 0.14″/yr.
    more

    On made-up images built like SPHEREx's (a star smaller than a pixel, a random roll each image, the same redrawing onto the grid), the star-centre step gets known motions right to within 0.5% and a still star at about 0, so it has no pixel bias of that size. What we found and changed, each on its own (all detectors; WISE 0855−0714 detectors 5–6):

    • Weighting images by how well they are measured. Images with a blanked, saturated centre are several times noisier and had counted the same as clean ones. Barnard's Star 9.75 → 10.32″/yr; Epsilon Indi B 4.37 → 4.38; WISE 0855−0714 6.90 → 7.15; RW Aurigae 0.57 → 0.51.
    • Taking out the yearly parallax wobble. With visits about every 6 months it alternates between seasons and leaks into a straight line. Epsilon Indi B 4.37 → 4.43″/yr; Barnard's Star 9.75 → 9.74 (its wobble is mostly across its motion); WISE 0855−0714 6.90 → 6.38; RW Aurigae no change.
    • Both, plus the 0.15″/yr floor in the error bar. Barnard's Star 10.32 ± 0.27″/yr (catalogue 10.39); Epsilon Indi B 4.46 ± 0.16″/yr (Gaia 4.68, 1.4σ); WISE 0855−0714 6.53 ± 1.98″/yr (catalogue 8.15); RW Aurigae 0.51 ± 0.17″/yr: no significant motion.

    Epsilon Indi B still sits 5% below Gaia. B is two brown dwarfs 0.7″ apart; their orbit moves the light centre by up to about 0.1″/yr, by different amounts in Gaia's visible light and SPHEREx's infrared. RW Aurigae's leftover drift is east–west; the field stars in the same images do not show it, so it is the young star's own light, or an effect of its bright centre that the floor does not cover.
