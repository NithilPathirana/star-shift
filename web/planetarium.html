What gets through the air

Pick something on the sky to see how high it is from each place now.

Air soaks up parts of the 0.74–5.01 µm light that SPHEREx sees, mostly through water vapour and carbon dioxide. Hatched = the air blocks it; clear = it gets through. The number on each band is how much gets through, on average.

Shading: ATRAN sky-transmission models (Lord, S. D. 1992, NASA Technical Memorandum 103957), courtesy of Gemini Observatory: Mauna Kea at 1.6 mm water vapour and Cerro Pachón at 2.3, 4.3 and 10 mm, looking straight up (airmass 1.0), averaged into 0.01 µm steps. Typical clear night for this height. Real nights change with water vapour.

How each row was chosen

    Only Mauna Kea has its own curve. The other places borrow the nearest real curve by height and water vapour, used as is: it is not rescaled, because the water, carbon dioxide and ozone parts of a curve cannot be separated. Heights above sea level from public site data. Target height and airmass (Kasten and Young 1989) are worked out in your browser for each place at the time shown above.
