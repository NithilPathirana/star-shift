IR band ranges

Name and colour your own wavelength ranges. SPHEREx covers 0.744–5.002 µm with 6 detectors (102 channels, 17 each). Dashed boxes show each detector's span.

Paint the sky map in these rangesChanges the sky map, not this time-lapse picture

Each SPHEREx image records one narrow wavelength at each spot. A range picks which of those wavelengths belong to it; with the switch on, the sky map draws those images in the range's colour and adds them together, so a place that is brighter in one range takes on that range's colour.

Each band is a wavelength range, not a detection: the viewer labels images "CO₂-ice band · 3.82–4.42 µm", meaning the light was measured in that range. In the time-lapse the picture itself stays grey; ranges colour the timeline ticks, the wavelength chip, the loading preview and the WAVE layer. Saved in this browser only.

Help: how to use Star Shift

Why this matters

“There will be too much data for any one person or even computer code to completely comprehend. […] We need your creative new tools to make the most of this treasure trove and help as many people as possible pitch in to make discoveries.” NASA, Space Apps 2026 challenge “Planet X and SPHEREx”

Volunteers comparing sky images have already found thousands of comets (SOHO’s Sungrazer project) and thousands of candidate brown dwarfs (Backyard Worlds: Planet 9). Star Shift lets anyone do the same with SPHEREx, and check a find against the catalogues before calling it new.

What each page is for

Viewer
Pick a star or a spot on the sky map, then flip through its SPHEREx images as a time-lapse to see what moved.
Archive
Every SPHEREx image of one patch of sky: dates, detectors, wavelengths and flagged pixels.
Spectrum
A quick-look infrared spectrum of one star, measured from single SPHEREx images.
Proof
For five well-known objects, what the catalogues expect next to what we measure, with honest limits.
Asteroids
Known asteroids and comets that NASA/JPL predicts inside a SPHEREx field on its dates.
Spot
Blink two seasons of one patch, click what jumps, and check it against flags, catalogues and JPL.
Can I see it tonight?
The sky from a place on Earth at a chosen time, to see whether a star is up.

Simple and Expert

Simple (the default) gives plain words, like "moves about 10 arcseconds a year". Turn on the Expert switch for the full detail: uncertainties (σ), χ², full coordinates, MJD dates, flag details and CSV export. The switch is remembered in this browser.

What the verdict chips mean

AGREES
Our measured speed is within 2 uncertainties (2σ) of the star catalogue.
AT THE LIMIT
Between 2σ and 3σ off, or the uncertainty is large: no firm answer either way.
DIFFERS
More than 3σ from the catalogue. Shown as measured, with the likely reason.
NOT MEASURABLE
The star moves too little over these dates (or its centre is blanked) for us to measure.

"No significant motion" means the measured shift is no bigger than its own error bars.

Can't load?

NASA's archive (IRSA) may be busy. Use the 5 NASA targets (Barnard's Star, Epsilon Indi B, WISE 0855, RW Aurigae, R Draconis): their images are saved with the app, so they open even when the archive is down.

About Star Shift

Every six months, NASA's SPHEREx photographs the whole sky. Star Shift lets anyone flip through those photos, catch what moved, and prove it's real.

Who is this for?

"Curious people, people that don't know and know about astronomy, people who are and aren't experienced in this field."

Our idea

"Using an image of a night sky that we can surf through and put coordinates so that it will point out a specific area and exactly show the light shifting shown by the infrared banding."

What we wanted it to do (our feature list)

  • "Go to RA/Dec and fetch night sky data, and allow panning in the website."
  • "Allow selecting a patch in the sky, and the website will calculate the exact coordinates, declination and all the data related and will present in the corner once clicked a box will open with this data."
  • "Allow the user to select a range of IR bands… choose a color to code the range and let the user name the range as they want."
  • "Allow the option of hovering over the stars so that the name would be displayed as well."
  • "Allow usage of user specific location when calculating coordinate data."
  • "Ghost – future predictions of stars, location."
  • "Velocity – of stars."
  • "Contamination flagging – air glow, cosmic rays, light scatter due to space dust, pixel overflow."
  • "JPL labeling."
  • "Live location based sky… option to take the night sky of major laboratories also and NASA centers also and enter coordinates to locate that sky too."
  • "Orbit comparison – ability to compare and distinguish based on different variables such as distance from Earth."
  • "Compare the images from multiple telescopes – to see how geographical position affects the night sky."
  • "Hypothesis creator notepad… add data values… with a + icon that is on everything."

— Team Proper Motion (Nithil, Kurucharan, Dehan)
